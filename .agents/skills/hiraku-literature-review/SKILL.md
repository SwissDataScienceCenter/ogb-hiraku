# Hiraku literature review

Use this procedure for paper discovery, literature reviews, related work, or
evidence synthesis. Focus on cited findings, limitations, and evidence-backed
gaps. Do not design hypotheses or experiments; leave that to the main agent or
a design specialist.

## Procedure

1. **Scope the question.** Use the user's request and relevant project records
   to establish the topic, boundaries, and desired depth. Choose an appropriate
   context record for the result.
2. **Search and select sources.** Use alphaXiv search when available. Vary
   searches meaningfully for broad questions, select relevant papers, and avoid
   describing targeted coverage as exhaustive. If alphaXiv is unavailable,
   disclose that and proceed only with user-provided papers or an explicitly
   requested preliminary synthesis from available sources. See the
   [alphaXiv MCP setup guide](https://www.alphaxiv.org/docs/mcp).
3. **Check central claims.** Retrieve paper text or focused PDF evidence where
   available. Keep each paper's evidence and citations distinct. Note when a
   paper or relevant evidence could not be checked.
4. **Synthesize evidence and gaps.** Organize findings by themes, methods,
   agreements, disagreements, and limitations. Separate source-supported
   findings from interpretation. Distinguish gaps in reviewed evidence from
   topics that may simply be outside the search coverage. Do not turn gaps into
   hypotheses or experiment plans.
5. **Write with citations.** Place citations beside supported claims, using
   bibliographic details and stable links. Preserve source citation markers
   when provided. State search coverage and limitations; do not invent details
   or results, and keep quotations brief.
6. **Save and verify.** Save the synthesis in a suitable existing Hiraku
   context record, or create one under the relevant research root. Check for ID
   conflicts first. When updating, preserve relevant existing content. Inspect
   the saved record to confirm its relationships and content.
